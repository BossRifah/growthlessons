I’d start with **Peec AI** if your team wants to track a defined set of buyer questions, see where competitors appear, and share results on a published monthly plan. I’d evaluate **Profound** if you also need to research what people ask AI engines and use those insights in recurring marketing workflows.

Both platforms cover more than many older comparisons suggest. Peec now offers [shopping analytics, crawler reporting, and an Agent](https://peec.ai/pricing). Profound’s current [brand pricing page](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing) lists a free trial and custom Enterprise pricing. Those details matter when you’re comparing the plan you can actually buy.

## Peec AI vs. Profound at a glance

| Buying question | Peec AI | Profound |
|---|---|---|
| What would I buy it for first? | Monitoring prompts and turning citation gaps into work for SEO, content, and PR teams | Connecting visibility monitoring with demand research and recurring marketing work |
| What does the brand entry offer cover? | Starter lists $95 per month, 50 prompts, a choice of three models, and one project | Free seven-day trial with 50 vendor-selected prompts and three specified engines |
| How can I research demand? | A relative demand score for the topic behind a tracked prompt | Prompt Volumes for exploring demand and finding related questions |

The biggest difference is how you buy and use each platform. Peec gives you a [priced monitoring setup](https://peec.ai/pricing) you can model on a spreadsheet. Profound gives you a [short trial](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing) to explore the product and a custom quote for ongoing brand use.

## What both tools measure

**AI search visibility** tells you how often a brand appears in a set of sampled answers. Your sample depends on the questions you track, which engines answer them, where you run them, and when you collect the responses. Keep that sample stable when you want to compare performance over time.

![media:17]()

*Peec AI’s sample dashboard shows how visibility, position, and sentiment can change across brands.*

Four signals answer different questions about the same buyer journey. I’d put each on its own line in a weekly report, with the time period and the part of the sample it covers.

| Signal | What to check | What to do with it |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand mention** | Whether the answer names your company and how it describes you | Read the answer to see whether the recommendation fits the buyer’s need. |
| **Citation** | Which page the answer visibly references | Decide whether to update your source page or seek a relevant third-party mention. |
| **Crawler visit** | Which bot requested a page and what response it received | Investigate blocked or failing requests on pages buyers need. |
| **Referral** | Whether your analytics recorded a visit from an AI platform | Check which landing pages receive those visits and what visitors do next. |

![media:14]()

*Track these four signals separately so each one points to a clear next step.*

For example, an answer can link to your guide while recommending a competitor’s product. The citation tells you your page was a visible source. Reading the recommendation tells you what the buyer was advised to choose. Some tools also report [source retrieval](https://help.peec.ai/article/why-does-a-link-on-google-ai-overview-not-count-as-a-mention-sp1qk4d9uf0eo9zf9pmblbnm), so ask which URLs were merely retrieved and which were displayed.

I’d start with questions your buyers ask when they shortlist, compare, and validate vendors. For a B2B software company, that might include alternatives to the market leader, an integration requirement, and pricing questions. The mix of prompts will look different if your goal is to understand [early awareness](https://growthlessons.vercel.app/blog/top-of-funnel-marketing-guide).

## Peec AI vs. Profound pricing

### What Peec AI costs for brands

Peec’s [US dollar brand pricing page](https://peec.ai/pricing) lists three monthly plans and a custom Enterprise option. The three public tiers each list a choice of three models and unlimited users. Starter includes CSV exports, Pro adds API access, and Advanced adds a Looker Studio connector.

| Peec plan | Price and billing | Tracked prompts | Projects | One extra model per month |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Starter | $95/month | 50 | 1 | $30 |
| Pro | $245/month | 150 | 2 | $70 |
| Advanced | $495/month | 350 | 5 | $140 |
| Enterprise | Custom, billed annually | Custom | Unlimited | Ask for the quoted coverage |

If you need a fourth eligible model on Starter, the listed base price and add-on total $125 per month. [Peec’s own example](https://peec.ai/pricing) counts 50 prompts across three models over 30 days as 4,500 answers. Those answers are repeated observations, so compare prompt coverage and collection frequency separately when you look at alternatives.

### What Profound costs for brands

Profound currently lists **Trial** and **Enterprise** on its [brand pricing page](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing). The free trial runs 50 prompts daily for seven days across ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews. The vendor says the trial’s prompt set is based on your industry and cannot be customized. Enterprise lists custom pricing, tailored prompt tracking, and support for up to ten answer engines.

If you’ve seen older $99 Starter or $399 Growth brand plans in comparisons, use the [current page](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing) and a written quote for your budget. Ask for the recurring amount, minimum term, included engines and markets, export access, and what happens if your team runs out of **agent credits**. Give the vendor an example of a real workflow so the credit estimate reflects its complexity.

### How I’d compare costs for an agency

Peec has a [separate agency pricing page](https://peec.ai/pricing-agencies) with a shared credit allowance. Its Essential plan lists $245 monthly for 10,000 credits. [Profound’s pricing FAQ](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing) also references an Agency Growth plan, with 400 agent credits per client workspace per month. Those figures measure different activity, so ask both vendors to quote your actual client list, engines, cadence, and reporting needs.

I’d test a small client and a larger one in the demo. Ask who can access each workspace, how client reporting works, and what happens to capacity when you add a client mid-month. Then calculate a typical monthly cost per active client and a second estimate for your busiest month.

“You can plug in any prompt and uncover how the client is being referenced,” said Niklas Buschner, founder and CEO of Radyant, in a [Peec customer story](https://peec.ai/blog/radyant-ai-search-visibility-case-study-peec-ai). I’d use the demo to see whether your team can get from that answer to a client recommendation with the evidence attached.

## Where the features differ in practice

### Use demand research to decide what to track

Peec gives the topic behind a tracked prompt a [relative demand score](https://peec.ai/pricing) from one to five. Profound’s **[Prompt Volumes](https://www.tryprofound.com/features/prompt-volumes)** offers research on what people ask and related demand. I’d use either source to prioritize topics, then check whether the suggested questions show the buying intent I’m interested in. Both the method used to estimate demand and the date range matter when you compare numbers.

![media:20]()

*Profound’s Conversation Volumes screen shows an example of how prompt demand estimates are broken down.*

If you already have useful questions in sales notes and support tickets, start there. Group them by product, buyer role, and decision stage, then choose the prompts that represent each group. For a fuller way to structure those topics before you load them into a tool, I’d use an [entity and relationship map](https://growthlessons.vercel.app/blog/how-to-find-search-entities).

### Turn a citation into work someone can own

Peec’s Actions feature suggests improvements based on visibility data, and [its Agent](https://peec.ai/product/agent) can investigate results and help act on them. Profound offers [reusable Agents and a Context Manager](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing) that supplies brand knowledge. Ask each vendor to take a real citation gap and turn it into a brief that a writer or PR lead could use. Judge the quality of the evidence and the edits your team would need to make.

![media:21]()

*This example from Profound shows how a content brief gathers citations and brand context before outlining.*

I’d give the evaluator a simple assignment. Find a buyer question where a competitor appears, open the answer and sources, and propose a [change to one existing page](https://growthlessons.vercel.app/blog/content-refresh-seo-ai-visibility). If the brief calls for outreach, check that it points to a relevant publication and gives a reason that a reader would benefit.

“We look to Agents to develop high quality V1 outputs that get routed to SMEs for review, reporting or implementation,” Fiona Erickson, MongoDB’s organic acquisition team lead, said in a [Profound customer story](https://www.tryprofound.com/customers/mongodb). Build that review time into your pilot, especially for a brief that needs technical sign-off.

### Make shopping and crawler analytics prove their value

Peec lists [catalog uploads and product-level visibility tracking](https://peec.ai/product/shopping) in its brand plan matrix. Profound offers shopping tracking, while its [brand trial excludes ChatGPT Shopping](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing). If you sell physical products, ask the vendor to show a product from your catalog in a current report and identify the engines, regions, and merchants that the quoted plan covers.

[Peec](https://peec.ai/product/agent-analytics) and [Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/features/agent-analytics) offer **crawler analytics** and AI referral reporting. Have your technical teammate bring one important URL and ask each vendor to show a recognized bot request, its status code, and where the data comes from. Ask separately how the tool detects referral visits. Your team can then decide whether it needs the platform’s technical reporting or can answer those questions with its existing analytics.

## A seven-day buying pilot

I’d use the pilot to judge the work your team can complete with each product. Before you start, pick one market, one person who will own the weekly report, and a small group of buyer questions. [Profound’s standard trial](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing) fixes the prompt set, so ask for a matched demo if you need a controlled comparison on your own questions.

1. **Set your buyer prompts.** Use real questions about categories, alternatives, integrations, and purchase criteria. Include both branded and unbranded prompts and write down which group each belongs to.
2. **Match engines and markets.** Compare the same question, engine surface, language, market, and time window where the tools let you. Keep Google AI Mode and AI Overviews separate in your notes. If the samples differ, assess each product’s workflow and record the difference alongside the scores.
3. **Inspect original answers.** Choose a handful of responses and follow their visible citations. Check whether the brand and its aliases were counted as expected, whether the cited URL matches the source shown, and whether you can explain a change to a colleague.
4. **Test reporting and costs.** Build the weekly report your team will actually send and try one useful action from it. Time how long the work takes, then use the production plan’s price, limits, and credits to estimate the monthly cost.

![media:15]()

*Use the same prompts and collection settings wherever the trial or demo allows.*

I’d score each step as **works today**, **requires a higher plan**, or **still unverified**. Make any must-have feature a purchase requirement. If a feature depends on a custom quote or a particular connector, ask the vendor to put it in writing before you sign.

## Which one should you buy?

![media:16]()

**Start with Peec AI** if you have a clear list of buyer prompts and your weekly job is to explain visibility changes, find citation opportunities, and share those findings with colleagues or clients. Its [public prices](https://peec.ai/pricing) give you a starting point for budgeting. Check the extra model and project costs once you know the coverage you need.

**Trial Profound** if demand research and repeatable marketing work are central to the purchase. Bring a real task you want its agents to perform, a sample brief, and the people who will use the output. Ask for the [ongoing quote](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing) and evaluate how often the workflow will run before comparing it with a Peec subscription.

If you still don’t have enough data to choose, write down the first decision you expect the platform to improve. Give both vendors the same job and ask them to demonstrate it on the plan you would buy. I’d choose the product that makes that decision easier for the person who will own it every week.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Peec AI cheaper than Profound?

Peec lists a [US brand Starter plan](https://peec.ai/pricing) at $95 per month. Profound lists a [free seven-day brand trial](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing) and custom Enterprise pricing, so compare your actual Profound quote with the Peec plan and add-ons you need.

### Can I compare their visibility scores directly?

A useful comparison requires the same prompts, engines, markets, time window, and metric definitions. Read sample responses to check how each tool handles mentions and citations, especially when a brand has several names or domains.

### Can I enter my own prompts in the Profound trial?

Profound says its [standard brand trial](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing) uses an industry-specific set of 50 prompts that you cannot edit. Ask for a matched demo if you need to evaluate a specific set of buyer questions.

### Do both platforms track AI shopping and crawler visits?

Yes, both vendors offer [shopping and crawler analytics](https://peec.ai/product/shopping). Verify the eligible engines, markets, products, and data sources on your plan, since [Profound’s standard trial](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing) excludes ChatGPT Shopping and integrations affect crawler data collection.

### How do I choose between them for an agency?

Get a quote based on your actual client workspaces, tracked prompts, markets, and reporting needs. Run through adding a client, sharing a report, and estimating overage costs so you can compare the plans using the same workload.
